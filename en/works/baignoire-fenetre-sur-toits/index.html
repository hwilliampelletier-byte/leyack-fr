Baignoire, fenêtre sur toits

Le Yack · 2026 · oil on canvas · 60 × 80 cm

Private collection

This painting has joined a private collection. A scene in the same spirit can be painted on commission.

Contact Le YackAsk for a commission

“The light comes in through the French window and stops halfway. That is more or less all I was after: for the bathroom to stay in its shadow, and for the city to be in full daylight. She holds her sponge against her shoulder; she is no longer washing, she is looking at the rooftops opposite. I do not know what she finds there. Perhaps nothing — just the fact that beyond the glass there is a building, windows, people getting up. We think a bath cuts us off; in truth, we are never as close to the street as when sitting in a bathtub, three metres from a balustrade. I left both casements open. Closed, it was a naked woman in a bathroom. Open, it is someone taking the air.”

— Le Yack

An oval bathtub in the middle of a room left in half-darkness, and facing it a French window open onto a wrought-iron balustrade, the pale façade opposite and its zinc roofs. All the light comes from there: it whitens the enamel, catches the brass of the taps and the sheet dropped over the rim, then fades into the browns of the corners.
